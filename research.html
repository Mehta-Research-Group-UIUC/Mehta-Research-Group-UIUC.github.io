Reinforcement Learning & Hidden Markov Models

What would a transformer look like if you derived it from first principles?

A language model predicts the next token from the ones before it. We model that setting with a hidden Markov model, where the observed tokens are generated by a hidden process, and treat next-token prediction as a filtering problem. Recasting the prediction as an optimal control problem yields the dual filter, an inference algorithm whose layer structure closely parallels a decoder-only transformer. Related work learns HMMs from data with filter-based networks.

On the learning side, we design systems of interacting particles that represent and learn optimal control laws for reinforcement learning. The interactions between particles greatly speed up convergence; a complete theory is developed for the linear quadratic case.

Particle & Nonlinear Filtering

How do you estimate what you cannot see, when nothing is linear or Gaussian?

Nonlinear filtering computes the conditional probability of a hidden state given noisy observations. Our best-known contribution is the feedback particle filter (FPF), which generalizes the Kalman filter to nonlinear, non-Gaussian problems. Instead of the importance weights and resampling of a conventional particle filter, each particle is moved by a feedback control law with the same structure as the Kalman filter’s update. The FPF belongs to a broader class of controlled interacting particle systems, related to the ensemble Kalman filter and to optimal transportation theory.

A parallel line of work develops the theory: a duality that expresses stochastic observability of a hidden Markov model as controllability of a dual control system, and uses it to study when a filter forgets a wrong initial guess (filter stability).

\[ dX^i_t = a(X^i_t)\,dt + \sigma(X^i_t)\,dB^i_t + \mathsf{K}(X^i_t,t)\circ\Big( dZ_t - \tfrac{1}{2}\big(h(X^i_t) + \hat h_t\big)\,dt \Big) \]

The feedback particle filter: particle \(i\) follows the model plus a correction driven by the observation \(Z_t\). The gain \(\mathsf{K}\) solves a Poisson equation.

Kalman Filtering & Stochastic Optimal Control

Why is the Riccati equation the fundamental equation of both estimation and control?

Kalman showed that, for linear systems, estimation and control are duals of each other: the covariance update of the Kalman filter, run with time reversed, is the Riccati equation of linear quadratic control. Extending that duality beyond the linear Gaussian model had been an open problem for decades. We presented a dual optimal control problem for nonlinear filtering, an exact extension of Kalman’s minimum variance duality.

The duality also runs the other way. The ensemble Kalman filter, built for estimation, can be turned into a dual ensemble Kalman filter that solves stochastic optimal control problems by simulation, linking this project to our work on reinforcement learning.

Soft Robotics

How do you control an arm with no joints and almost infinite degrees of freedom?

An octopus arm is hyper-flexible, yet coordinates a rich repertoire of motions. We modeled a soft arm as a Cosserat rod with biomechanically realistic muscles, and used energy shaping control to make it reach, grasp and crawl, both in simulation (CyberOctopus) and on the BR2 soft robot. A stored energy function for the muscles avoids the hardest step of energy shaping, solving the matching conditions. A sensory feedback control law reproduced the bend propagation seen in real octopus arms.

The work was part of a multi-university MURI project with collaborators across Illinois.

Epidemiological Modeling

What can control theory say about the spread of a disease?

When COVID-19 spread around the world, the group joined the effort with collaborators at the Coordinated Science Laboratory, including Tamer Başar and Carolyn Beck, working on modeling, analysis and control of disease spread with support from the C3.ai Digital Transformation Institute. Stochastic models based on jump processes were developed for both epidemiology and neuroscience.

Sensorimotor Control

How do sensing and movement work together in a body without a skeleton?

Neural models and algorithms for sensorimotor control of an octopus arm: how sensory signals along the arm can be turned into coordinated muscle activity, drawing on observations by biologists. This work grew into the soft robotics project.

Neural Rhythms

What do coupled oscillators tell us about rhythms in the brain?

Populations of neurons fire in rhythms. We used models of coupled oscillators to study how such rhythms arise, synchronize and interact, including coupling across frequencies. The same mathematics drives the Kuramoto animation on the Team page.

Mean Field Games

What happens when very many players each optimize for themselves?

Games with very large numbers of interacting players, analyzed through their mean-field limit. A key result was that synchronization of coupled oscillators can be understood as a game, with a phase transition between incoherence and synchrony. Mean-field ideas continue in the group’s interacting particle algorithms for filtering and control.
