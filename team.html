Principal investigator

Prashant G. Mehta

Prashant Mehta is a Professor in the Department of Mechanical Science and Engineering at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC). At Illinois, he holds affiliate appointments in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, the Coordinated Science Laboratory, and the Neuroscience Program. He received his Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from Cornell University in 2004.

Before joining Illinois in 2005, he was a Research Engineer at the United Technologies Research Center (UTRC), where he developed a method to suppress combustion instabilities in jet engines and afterburners, a sixty-year-old problem. The method has since become an industry standard in Pratt & Whitney engines. He co-founded Rithmio, a software company where he served as Chief Science Officer, whose technology portfolio was acquired by Bosch Sensortec in 2017.

His research group is known for the Feedback Particle Filter algorithm, now widely used for nonlinear filtering, and more recently for work on duality, stochastic observability, and nonlinear filter stability. His students have won the Best Student Paper Award at the IEEE Conference on Decision and Control in 2007, 2009, and 2019, and were finalists at CDC 2012 and ACC 2010. He currently serves on the Awards Board of the IEEE Control Systems Society and as a Senior Editor of the IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control. He has previously served on the editorial boards of Systems & Control Letters and the ASME Journal of Dynamic Systems, Measurement, and Control. He is a Fellow of IEEE and ASME.

Graduate students

Aditya Kudre

Ph.D. student, ECE

Language modeling through nonlinear filtering and optimal control. With Heng-Sheng, he uses hidden Markov models and their higher-order variants to study token prediction and transformer-like architectures.

Website akudre2@illinois.edu

Jiangjun (Alex) Yu

Ph.D. student

Mean-field games and neural algorithms for large interacting systems. He studies collective dynamics and cross-frequency coupling with oscillator models and McKean–Vlasov formulations.

LinkedIn jy48@illinois.edu

Anant Joshi

Ph.D. student

Log transform duality for stochastic control and reinforcement learning. The log transform turns a cost function into a probability density, which an interacting particle system then samples. Inspired by the ensemble Kalman filter, his algorithms are sample efficient compared with benchmark methods on high-dimensional problems.

Website anantaj2@illinois.edu
